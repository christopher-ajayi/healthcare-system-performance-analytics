# Resource-Effectiveness Framework

High expenditure must **not** automatically be interpreted as better performance.

Jurisdictions will be examined using descriptive resource-performance patterns:

| Resource Level | Performance | Interpretation                           |
| -------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| High           | Strong      | High-resource / strong-performance       |
| High           | Weak        | Potential resource-effectiveness concern |
| Low            | Strong      | Potential resource effectiveness         |
| Low            | Weak        | Potential resource constraint            |

> **Important:** These categories describe observed relationships. They do not establish causality or prove technical efficiency.

# Comparative Analysis Principles

All provincial and territorial comparisons must:

* [ ] Distinguish absolute measures from population-adjusted measures.
* [ ] Account for differences in population size.
* [ ] Consider demographic composition.
* [ ] Avoid simplistic league tables.
* [ ] Recognize geographic and territorial differences.
* [ ] Consider differences in healthcare delivery environments.
* [ ] Compare jurisdictions over comparable periods.
* [ ] Avoid interpreting correlation as causation.
* [ ] Identify structural differences where possible.
* [ ] Use Canada-level comparisons where analytically appropriate.

# Applied Research Principles

Every analysis must answer **three questions**:

### 1. What happened?

Describe the observed pattern.

### 2. Why does it matter economically or systemically?

Interpret the pattern in terms of:

* demand
* resource allocation
* capacity
* fiscal pressure
* access
* outcomes
* sustainability

### 3. What does the evidence suggest?

Develop a cautious, evidence-based interpretation.

---

# Analytical Discipline

Every SQL query, statistical analysis, visualization and interpretation must satisfy at least one of the following:

* [ ] Answers a research question.
* [ ] Answers an analytical sub-question.
* [ ] Produces a metric required to answer a research question.
* [ ] Tests a relationship between research dimensions.
* [ ] Validates data required for the research.
* [ ] Supports an evidence-based interpretation.

**No analysis should be performed merely because the dataset contains the variable.**

---

# Causal Interpretation Rule

This project is primarily **observational and descriptive/analytical**.

Therefore:

* [ ] Do not claim that expenditure causes better outcomes.
* [ ] Do not claim that fewer beds cause longer wait times.
* [ ] Do not claim that demographic ageing causes a specific outcome without appropriate causal methodology.
* [ ] Use language such as **associated with**, **corresponds with**, **coincides with**, **is consistent with**, or **may indicate**.
* [ ] Reserve causal claims for analyses that actually support causal inference.

# Final Research Objective

The ultimate objective is to determine whether Canadian provincial and territorial healthcare systems demonstrate evidence of:

> **alignment or misalignment between changing healthcare demand, financial resources, physical and human capacity, access, and outcomes.**

The analysis should identify:

* [ ] Where healthcare demand is increasing.
* [ ] Whether resources are keeping pace.
* [ ] Whether capacity is keeping pace.
* [ ] Whether access is deteriorating or improving.
* [ ] Whether outcomes are improving or deteriorating.
* [ ] Whether greater resources correspond with stronger performance.
* [ ] Where persistent resource-demand mismatches exist.
* [ ] Where systems appear comparatively resource-effective.
* [ ] Where sustainability pressures appear greatest.
